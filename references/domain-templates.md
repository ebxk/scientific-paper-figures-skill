# Domain templates

Everything in this file is [Domain Template]. These are candidate evidence chains, not required figures, fixed counts, or venue rules. Keep only items that trace to the manuscript's claims and available evidence.

## Applied statistics, economics, and finance

Candidate sequence:

1. setting/data construction or identification diagram when needed;
2. descriptive distribution or time/market context;
3. primary estimate/comparison with uncertainty;
4. heterogeneity, sensitivity, or regime analysis;
5. mechanism/ablation where scientifically justified;
6. robustness, placebo, leakage, or alternative specification.

Common Main candidates: primary estimate, economically meaningful comparison, decisive sensitivity, essential identification/method diagram.

Common Appendix candidates: cleaning thresholds, full feature definitions, complete specification grids, seed/window diagnostics, secondary subgroups, all numerical estimates.

Do not default to cumulative-return curves, dual axes, correlation heatmaps, or feature importance unless they answer a stated research question.

## Machine learning and AI

Candidate sequence:

1. task and method overview;
2. architecture detail only for the novel component;
3. primary benchmark comparison with uncertainty/variation;
4. ablation tied to contributions;
5. robustness, scaling, calibration, or failure analysis;
6. qualitative examples selected by a declared protocol.

Keep implementation plumbing out of the main architecture figure unless the infrastructure itself is the contribution. Distinguish a method pipeline from a layer-by-layer network diagram.

## Experimental and laboratory research

Candidate sequence:

1. experimental design and groups;
2. representative image/trace plus quantitative summary;
3. primary outcome with biological/technical replicates distinguished;
4. mechanism or perturbation evidence;
5. replication, dose/time response, or boundary conditions.

For image panels, retain originals, scale information, acquisition conditions, crop/manipulation history, and consistent comparison treatment.

## Observational and clinical research

Candidate sequence:

1. cohort flow or inclusion/exclusion diagram;
2. baseline characteristics table;
3. primary association/effect with uncertainty;
4. subgroup or interaction display;
5. calibration/discrimination or sensitivity analysis where relevant;
6. missing-data and attrition diagnostics.

Do not imply causality through arrows or titles unless the design and analysis support a causal claim.

## Dataset, benchmark, or software resource

Candidate sequence:

1. construction pipeline;
2. coverage/composition and quality checks;
3. benchmark or validation;
4. representative use case;
5. limitations and failure modes.

## Review or conceptual paper

Candidate sequence:

1. scope/taxonomy;
2. conceptual framework or mechanism map;
3. evidence landscape;
4. unresolved gaps and future research agenda.

Avoid manufacturing quantitative-looking marks when the source evidence is qualitative or heterogeneous.
