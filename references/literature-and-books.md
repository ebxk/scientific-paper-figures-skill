# Literature and books

Use these sources to ground general design and planning principles. A scholarly recommendation is not automatically a journal requirement.

## Papers and tutorials

- Rougier, Droettboom & Bourne, [Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833). Audience/message first; medium, captions, defaults, color, misleading encodings, chartjunk, and tool choice.
- Jambor & Bornhäuser, [Ten Simple Rules for Designing Graphical Abstracts](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011789). Reading direction, visual grammar, iteration, and feedback for graphical abstracts; do not present every point as a universal structure-diagram rule.
- American Physiological Society, [Publishing Particulars, Part 2](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpregu.00267.2022). Manuscript-development storyboard for ordering key results, figures, and tables.
- PLOS, [Ten Simple Rules for Typographically Appealing Scientific Texts](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008458). General scientific typography including mathematical notation.
- Wilke, [Fundamentals of Data Visualization](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/). Open online book covering distributions, time series, relationships, uncertainty, color, coordinates, and multi-panel figures.

## Books and publisher pages

- Yale University Press, [Visual Strategies](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300176445/visual-strategies/), Frankel & DePace. Scientific structure, process, comparison, and evidence.
- Bloomsbury, [How to Write and Publish a Scientific Paper](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/how-to-write-and-publish-a-scientific-paper-9781440878824/), Gastel & Day. Distinct roles of tables, graphs, and other visuals.
- Columbia University Press, [Better Data Visualizations](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/better-data-visualizations/9780231550154/), Schwabish. Task-oriented chart selection and communication.
